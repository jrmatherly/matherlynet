Every internal model call at The Aaron's Company, a 15,000-person retailer, goes through one gateway. Claude Code,
Claude Desktop, our agents and the people using our internal tools all reach Azure AI Foundry and Anthropic through it.
I designed it, then rebuilt it twice in eighteen months.

## Quick facts

- **Role:** architect and lead engineer; Manager, Infrastructure Services. My four-engineer team runs it with me.
- **Timeline:** December 2024 to June 2026; since June 2026, in-house on a customized fork of TokenHub
- **Scale:** from about 15 million tokens a month at launch to 6B+ by October 2026
- **Cost:** projected licensing from $100K+ a year to about $38K, then to near zero

## The problem

The first version was a quick way to give teams safe access to models. Demand showed up immediately, and so did
needs that no product met out of the box, such as cost tracked per team and user and MCP server sign-in through
Entra ID. The first two generations were retired for the same reason: each still needed custom work on top of a
license fee.

## Generation 1: LiteLLM (December 2024)

<GatewayMap generation="litellm" />

LiteLLM was fast to stand up and proved the demand. It needed custom work for two things: per-team and per-user cost
attribution, and MCP server OAuth with Entra ID. Its licensing was projected at more than $100,000 a year.

## Generation 2: Azure API Management (February 2026)

<GatewayMap generation="apim" />

Moving to Azure API Management added single sign-on and rate limits, and cut projected licensing to about $38,000 a
year. But every capability the business asked for next became a custom integration, and those were bounded by
what Azure allows.

## Choosing to build

Before building the third generation I evaluated Portkey, Kong AI Gateway, Envoy, Agent Gateway and kgateway. Each
one either lacked features we needed or cost too much. If we had to modify a product, we did not want to pay for it
too. Bringing the gateway in-house on an open-source base gave us full control at a licensing cost close to zero.

We forked TokenHub, an Apache-licensed open-source gateway, because it didn't offer what we needed out of the box: JIT
provisioning, Entra ID support, token caching, LLM costs that vary with short or long context and cache windows, cost
tracking and budgets per group and team, and an MCP Gateway, Registry and Portal. We maintain the fork as AIOpsHub.

## Generation 3: in-house (June 2026)

<GatewayPath />

The current gateway runs on our on-prem Kubernetes platform, managed through GitOps. Every request:

- signs in with SSO, so cost is tracked per user and per team;
- is rate limited;
- passes guardrails that check for personal data, secrets, prompt injection and restricted topics;
- can be answered from a cache instead of a model;
- is routed to Azure AI Foundry or Anthropic;
- is written to an audit log kept for at least 90 days and reviewed by audit and compliance.

I wrote the enterprise layer on top of the fork: Entra group-to-quota provisioning, cache-aware routing, cost
reconciled against provider bills, and OpenTelemetry tracing. The guardrails block a few hundred requests a day, and
model fallback kept service up through a provider outage. Information Security and audit approved the controls.

Tools sit behind a separate MCP Gateway, which reaches only the MCP servers approved into our registry, over OAuth.

## Results

| Metric | Before | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tokens a month | about 15 million (launch) | 6B+ |
| Projected licensing | $100K+ a year (LiteLLM) | near zero |

## What I'd do differently

Talk to the business units sooner. The first two generations were replaced after needs surfaced that they could
meet only with custom work; asking earlier would have found some of those needs before I built around them.
